- [S1] OSHA — Stop Falls / Fall Prevention Campaign — https://www.osha.gov/stop-falls
  Claim: "FALLS ARE THE LEADING CAUSE OF DEATH IN CONSTRUCTION. In 2024, there were 389 fatal falls to a lower level out of 1,034 construction fatalities (BLS data)." Plan-Provide-Train campaign framing; partnership with NIOSH/NORA since 2012. Supports: gap significance, fall-protection topic stat and key-point framing.

- [S2] OSHA — Fall Protection Overview — https://www.osha.gov/fall-protection
  Claim: OSHA requires fall protection at 4 ft (general industry), 5 ft (shipyards), **6 ft (construction)**, 8 ft (longshoring); regardless of height over dangerous equipment; guard floor holes; train in a language workers understand. Supports: fall-protection topic 6-ft trigger and guardrail/hole-guarding points.

- [S3] OSHA — 29 CFR 1926.501, Duty to have fall protection — https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.501
  Claim: §1926.501(b)(1): employees on walking/working surfaces with unprotected sides/edges **6 ft or more** above a lower level must be protected by guardrail systems, safety net systems, or personal fall arrest systems; (b)(4) covers/skylights; (b)(7) excavation edges ≥6 ft; (c) falling objects → hard hat + toeboards/canopy/barricades. Supports: fall-protection topic source_ref and key points.

- [S4] OSHA — 29 CFR 1926.502, Fall protection systems criteria — https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.502
  Claim: guardrail top rail 42 in ±3 in; top rail 200 lb / midrail 150 lb strength; safety nets ≤30 ft below work surface; PFAS anchorage ≥5,000 lb per employee (d)(15); lanyards/lifelines 5,000 lb (d)(9); locking snaphooks only since Jan 1 1998 (d)(5); body belts not acceptable in PFAS since Jan 1 1998; max arresting force 1,800 lb with harness (d)(16)(ii). Supports: fall-protection topic harness/anchorage/guardrail points.

- [S5] OSHA — 29 CFR 1926.451, Scaffolds general requirements — https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.451
  Claim: capacity ≥4× intended load (a)(1); full planking with gaps ≤1 in (b)(1)(i); platform ≥18 in wide (b)(2); 4:1 height-to-base requires guying/tying/bracing (c)(1); base plates + mud sills (c)(2); **competent person inspects for visible defects before each work shift and after anything affecting integrity (f)(3)**; erection/dismantling under a competent person (f)(7); no work on snow/ice-covered scaffolds (f)(8); no boxes/barrels/ladders on platforms to gain height (f)(14)-(f)(15); power-line clearance 10 ft <50 kV (f)(6); **fall protection for scaffold work more than 10 ft above a lower level (g)(1)**. Supports: scaffold-safety topic.

- [S6] OSHA — Scaffolding topic page — https://www.osha.gov/scaffolding
  Claim: scaffold hazards = falls, falling objects, scaffold collapses; scissor-lift hazard alert (OSHA 3842). Supports: scaffold-safety summary angle.

- [S7] OSHA — Trenching and Excavation topic page — https://www.osha.gov/trenching-excavation
  Claim: "Trench collapses, or cave-ins, pose the greatest risk to workers' lives"; prevent via SLOPE / SHORE / SHIELD; safe entry/exit; keep materials off the edge; watch water/atmosphere; "Never enter a trench unless it has been properly inspected"; standards 1926.650–652; National Emphasis Program CPL 02-00-161. Supports: trench topic framing and Slope/Shore/Shield point.

- [S8] OSHA — 29 CFR 1926.652, Requirements for protective systems — https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.652
  Claim: cave-in protection required in every excavation except stable rock or **depths under 5 ft where a competent person's examination shows no cave-in indication (a)(1)(ii)**; Option 1 slope max 1.5H:1V / 34° (b)(1)(i); shield systems must restrict lateral movement; **no employees inside shields during vertical install/remove/move (g)(1)(iv)**. Supports: trench topic 5-ft rule and shield points.

- [S9] OSHA — 29 CFR 1926.651, Specific excavation requirements — https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.651
  Claim: locate/contact underground utilities before digging (b); ladder/egress within 25 ft lateral travel at ≥4 ft (c)(2); hi-vis vests near public traffic (d); **no employee under loads handled by lifting or digging equipment (e)**; warning systems for mobile equipment near excavation edges (f); atmospheric testing >4 ft when hazard suspected (g)(1)(i); spoil ≥2 ft from edge (j)(2); **daily competent-person inspections before work starts, as needed during shift, and after every rainstorm (k)(1)**; employees removed from the hazard until precautions taken (k)(2). Supports: trench topic competent-person/egress/spoil/line-of-fire points.

- [S10] OSHA Alert 3971 (2022) — Trench collapses can be deadly — https://www.osha.gov/sites/default/files/publications/OSHA3971.pdf
  Claim: "Trench collapses, or cave-ins, are more likely than other excavation-related incidents to result in worker fatalities"; "It only takes seconds to become buried in thousands of pounds of soil"; Slope/Shore/Shield; never enter an uninspected trench. Supports: trench topic qualitative stat line.

- [S12] OSHA — 29 CFR 1926.404 — https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.404

Supports: electrical-jobsite GFCI and cord-inspection points.
