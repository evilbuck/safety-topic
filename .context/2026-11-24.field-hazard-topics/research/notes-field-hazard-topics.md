## 2. Primary-source fetch log (OSHA / BLS)

- **osha.gov/stop-falls** — verbatim: "FALLS ARE THE LEADING CAUSE OF DEATH IN CONSTRUCTION. In 2024, there were 389 fatal falls to a lower level out of 1,034 construction fatalities (BLS data)." Plan-Provide-Train framing confirmed (plan the job / provide right equipment — ladders, scaffolds, PFAS — / train everyone). Campaign partnership with NIOSH/NORA since 2012.
- **osha.gov/fall-protection** — verbatim: fall protection "at elevations of four feet in general industry workplaces, five feet in shipyards, six feet in the construction industry and eight feet in longshoring operations"; fall protection over dangerous equipment "regardless of the fall distance"; train workers "in a language that they can understand".
- **1926.501(b)(1)** — unprotected sides/edges ≥6 ft ⇒ guardrail systems, safety net systems, or personal fall arrest systems. (b)(4): covers for holes incl. skylights; (b)(7): excavation edges ≥6 ft; (c): falling objects ⇒ hard hat + toeboards/canopy/barricade.
- **1926.502** — top rail 42 in ±3 in; top rail holds 200 lb, midrail 150 lb; safety net ≤30 ft below work level; PFAS anchorage ≥5,000 lb per employee (d)(15); lanyards/vertical lifelines 5,000 lb (d)(9); only locking snaphooks since Jan 1 1998 (d)(5); body belts not acceptable as part of a PFAS since Jan 1 1998; max arresting force 1,800 lb with body harness (d)(16)(ii).
- **1926.451** — capacity 4× intended load (a)(1); full planking, gaps ≤1 in (b)(1)(i); platform ≥18 in wide (b)(2); 4:1 height-to-base ⇒ guys/ties/braces (c)(1); base plates + mud sills (c)(2); **competent person inspects for visible defects before each work shift** (f)(3); erection/dismantling only under competent person (f)(7); no work on snow/ice-covered scaffolds (f)(8); no boxes/barrels or ladders on platforms to gain height (f)(14)/(f)(15); power-line clearance 10 ft under 50 kV (f)(6); **fall protection for employees on scaffolds more than 10 ft above a lower level** (g)(1).
- **osha.gov/scaffolding** — hazards: falls, falling objects, scaffold collapses; scissor-lift hazard alert OSHA 3842.
- **osha.gov/trenching-excavation** — "Trench collapses, or cave-ins, pose the greatest risk"; Slope It / Shore It / Shield It; standards 1926.650/651/652; National Emphasis Program CPL 02-00-161 (2018); 2026 Trench Safety Stand Down June 15–19 (NUCA).
- **1926.652(a)(1)** — protective systems required except stable rock, or **less than 5 ft** deep with competent-person exam showing no cave-in indication; Option 1 max slope 1.5H:1V (34°) (b)(1)(i); shields must restrict lateral movement; **employees not allowed in shields when being installed/removed/moved vertically** (g)(1)(iv).
- **1926.651** — utility locates before opening excavation (b); egress at ≥4 ft trenches within 25 ft lateral travel (c)(2); hi-vis vests near public traffic (d); **no employee under loads handled by lifting or digging equipment** (e); warning systems (barricades/signals/stop logs) for mobile equipment near excavation edges (f); atmosphere test >4 ft when hazard suspected (g)(1)(i); spoil ≥2 ft from edge (j)(2); **daily competent-person inspections before start of work, as needed during shift, after every rainstorm** (k)(1); employees removed from hazard until precautions taken (k)(2).
- **OSHA Alert 3971 (2022)** — "Trench collapses, or cave-ins, are more likely than other excavation-related incidents to result in worker fatalities"; "It only takes seconds to become buried in thousands of pounds of soil."
- **1926 Subpart K listing** — 1926.403 general; 1926.404 wiring design and protection; 1926.416 general (safety-related work practices); 1926.417 lockout and tagging of circuits.
- **1926.404(b)(1)** — GFCIs required on all 120 V single-phase 15/20 A receptacles on construction sites not part of permanent wiring; alternative: assured equipment grounding conductor program — written program on site, competent person designated, **visual inspection of cords/plugs before each day's use**, continuity + correct-ground tests before first use / after repair / after suspected damage / ≤3 months (6 months fixed undamaged), test records by log or color coding.
- **osha.gov/training/outreach/construction/focus-four** — Construction Focus Four = **falls, caught-in or -between, struck-by, electrocution**; lesson plans are the "Required Curriculum" for OSHA construction outreach trainers.
- **constrfocusfour_introduction.pdf (OTI, April 2011)** — Focus Four module "mandatory for both the 10- and 30-hour" construction outreach courses; 10-hour: falls ≥1 h 15 min **"Because most construction fatalities are caused by fall hazards"**, other three ≥30 min each; per-hazard lesson structure (definition / major types / how to protect / employer duties).
- **BLS CFOI news release, 2024 data (USDL-26-0230, Feb 19 2026)** — 5,070 total fatal work injuries (−4.0%); rate 3.3/100k; transportation incidents most frequent fatal event, 1,937 (38.2%); falls/slips/trips 844; **construction and extraction occupations: 1,032 fatalities; falls/slips/trips among them 370**.
- **BLS CFOI Table 2 (2023–24)** — struck by propelled/falling/suspended object **357**; struck/caught/compressed by running powered equipment **213**; exposure to electricity **130**; collapse/engulfment **80**; pedestrian struck by vehicle in road work zone **61**; fall to lower level **666**; contact incidents total **756**.
- **osha.gov/control-hazardous-energy** — hazardous energy = electrical/mechanical/hydraulic/pneumatic/chemical/thermal; unexpected startup or stored-energy release can kill; injuries "electrocution, burns, crushing, cutting, lacerating, amputating"; craft workers among greatest risk; standard = **1910.147** (general industry); LOTO also addressed in construction standards incl. **1926 Subpart K**, Subpart Q, Subpart V.
- **1926.300** — tools "maintained in a safe condition" (a); guards required on power tools designed to accommodate them (b)(1); moving parts (belts, pulleys, sprockets…) guarded (b)(2); point-of-operation guarding for shears/presses/saws/portable power tools (b)(4); **constant-pressure switch required for circular saws, chain saws, percussion tools** (d)(3); PPE per subparts D/E (c).
- **1926.453** — aerial lifts: lift controls **tested each day prior to use** (b)(2)(i); only authorized persons operate (b)(2)(ii); **no belting off to adjacent pole/structure** (b)(2)(iii); stand firmly on basket floor, no planks/ladders (b)(2)(iv); body belt + lanyard attached to boom or basket (b)(2)(v); load limits (b)(2)(vi); brakes set, outriggers on pads, wheel chocks on incline (b)(2)(vii); **do not move truck with boom elevated** unless designed for it (b)(2)(viii).
