# Research: toolbox-talk documentation requirements

## Summary

SafetyTopic serves the brief but stops before the **record**. Federal OSHA does not mandate employee-signed sign-in sheets, but training standards expect an employer/trainer-signed certification record (1997/2000 interpretation letters), and OSHA partnership agreements require documented weekly toolbox talks. California 8 CCR §3203(b)(2) outright requires documented training (employee identifier, dates, type, provider, ≥1 year retention). Insurers, GCs, and safety auditors treat these records as the practical proof a talk happened. Every surveyed template (SAIF, SALUS, Workyard, ConstructionBids, TailgateDocs) and every surveyed app (Safesite, Safetymint, TailgateDocs) fills the same gap: a one-page record with header fields, attendance signatures, attestation, follow-up notes, and presenter sign-off.

## Key findings

1. **Documentation is the practical proof.** OSHA verifies training happened and was understandable (2010 policy statement); records are how employers demonstrate it.
2. **Conventional sign-off sheet fields** (convergent across all templates):
   - Header: date, topic title, presenter, location/job site, company, duration (min)
   - Attendance: printed name + signature per row (~10–15 rows), with an attestation line ("By signing I confirm I attended this safety talk and understood its content.")
   - Footer: notes/follow-up actions, presenter signature + date — the one signature OSHA actually expects.
3. **App landscape**: record flow = conduct talk → capture attendance → export/print PDF; history filterable by date/topic.

## Recommended minimal fields (for SafetyTopic sign-off sheet)

Header: Date (prefilled), Topic (from data), Presented by ___, Location/Crew ___, Company ___, Duration (from topic data). Attendance: 12 rows Name (print) / Signature + attestation line. Footer: Notes/follow-up lines, Presenter signature + date line. Print output: one page.

## Recommendation

One major change: **"Safety Talk Record"** — a printable sign-off sheet mode (brief + header + attendance + attestation + presenter sign-off) plus a dated local log of delivered talks ("I Used This" becomes a real record). This converts the app from a topic dispenser into a run-and-document meeting opener, matching the purpose claim in the README.
